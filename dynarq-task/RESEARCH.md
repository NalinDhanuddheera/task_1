1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer:Statutory interest for late payment is simple interest at a rate equal to the reference rate plus at least eight percentage points (Source 1, Article 2(6)). For a euro Member State the reference rate is either the rate the European Central Bank applied to its most recent main refinancing operations, or the marginal rate from variable-rate tenders for those operations (Source 1, Article 2(7)(a)). The rate in force on 1 January is used for the first half of the year and the rate in force on 1 July for the second half (Source 1, Article 3(2)).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer:
   The sources do not support it. They contain no 25 EUR maximum. The only
   fixed sum is a minimum of EUR 40 for recovery costs, which becomes payable when interest for late payment is payable (Source 1, Article 6(1)). On top of that the creditor can claim reasonable compensation for recovery costs above the fixed sum (Source 1, Article 6(3)). A term that excludes compensation for recovery costs is presumed grossly unfair (Source 1, Article 7(3)). 3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer:No. Between businesses, the creditor is entitled to interest "without the necessity of a reminder" if it fulfilled its own obligations and did not receive
   the amount on time, unless the debtor is not responsible for the delay (Source 1, Article 3(1)). Interest runs from the day after the payment date fixed in the contract (Source 1, Article 3(3)(a)). The fixed sum is also payable without a reminder (Source 1, Article 6(2)). So the owner's belief that interest needs a reminder first is not what the directive says. Contract terms and national law are not in the sources
